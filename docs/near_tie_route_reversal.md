# Near-tie route selection is a singular perturbation problem

The exact shared-route leading dynamics predict that, for

\[
L_2=-a\,xy-b\,xz,
\]

zero exits grow in the coefficient-aligned direction. In particular, if
`a>b>0`, the quadratic leading model predicts that route `00` reaches every
common small threshold before route `01`.

That statement is pointwise correct away from coefficient ties. It is **not
uniformly correct near a tie surface** once the full finite-memory loss contains
higher-order terms.

This note freezes an exact finite-memory counterexample and gives the local
scaling law for the width of the ambiguous near-tie region.

## Local rescaling

Let the first nonconstant loss degree be `d`, and let the first correction able
to change the relevant route competition have degree `p>d`:

\[
L(x;\lambda)=L_d(x;\lambda)+L_p(x;\lambda)+\cdots.
\]

Write

\[
x=\delta y,
\qquad
t=\delta^{2-d}s.
\]

Then the rescaled gradient flow is generated by

\[
\widetilde L(y;\lambda,\delta)
=
L_d(y;\lambda)
+
\delta^{p-d}L_p(y;\lambda)
+
\cdots.
\]

More generally, every degree-`r` monomial receives the exact factor

\[
\delta^{r-d}.
\]

`rescaled_gradient_flow_loss_coefficients` implements this coefficient
rescaling directly for sparse exact polynomials.

## Near-tie window

Suppose two leading routes tie at a parameter value `lambda_star`, and their
leading-strength difference opens transversely:

\[
\Delta a(\lambda)
=
K(\lambda-\lambda_*)+O((\lambda-\lambda_*)^2),
\qquad K\ne0.
\]

If the parameter is tuned on the scale

\[
\lambda-\lambda_*
=
\kappa\,\delta^{p-d},
\]

then the leading-route asymmetry and the degree-`p` correction both enter the
rescaled vector field at the same order:

\[
O(\delta^{p-d}).
\]

Therefore the route selected by `L_d` alone is not uniformly protected inside a
window of width

\[
\boxed{
|\lambda-\lambda_*|=O(\delta^{p-d}).
}
\]

This is the route-selection analogue of the approximate-invariant lifetime law.
The same degree gap `p-d` controls how close to a leading degeneracy one must be
before the higher-order computation geometry becomes first-order relevant.

`near_tie_margin_exponent(d,p)` returns this exponent.

This scaling argument is a standard singular-perturbation/Taylor-rescaling idea;
we do not claim that generic mathematical mechanism as novel. The finite-memory
contribution is the exact computation-route realization and its frozen
coefficient-level reversal.

## Exact finite-memory witness

Use the non-delayed second-order Markov source already used in the shared-route
results:

\[
P(1\mid00)=0.1,
\quad
P(1\mid01)=0.8,
\quad
P(1\mid10)=0.6,
\quad
P(1\mid11)=0.2.
\]

One learned entrance transition reaches an intermediate memory after symbol `0`.
Two learned exits then recognize suffixes `00` and `01`.

The `01` decoder remains exact:

\[
q_{01}=0.8.
\]

Let `q` be the decoder probability of symbol `1` in the `00` memory. The leading
route-`00` strength is

\[
a(q)
=
\frac{10}{21}
\left[
\log 2
+0.9\log(1-q)
+0.1\log q
\right].
\]

The exact `01` strength is

\[
b
=
\frac{5}{42}
\left[
\log 2-H(0.8)
\right]
=
0.02294580440735208\ldots.
\]

There is a unique useful-side tie near uniform readout at

\[
\boxed{
q_*=0.46869740167476925\ldots
}
\]

for which

\[
a(q_*)=b.
\]

At this same tie, the exact degree-three coefficients multiplying the
route-specific monomials are

\[
[x^2y]L_3=0.81b,
\qquad
[x^2z]L_3=0.36b.
\]

Thus the cubic slowdown is substantially stronger for route `00`.

## Exact initial-gradient reversal

Set

\[
q=q_*-\kappa\delta.
\]

Because `a'(q_*)<0`, every fixed positive `kappa` makes

\[
a(q)>b
\]

for sufficiently small `delta`: the quadratic shared-route model therefore
predicts route `00`.

At the initial point

\[
(x,y,z)=(\delta,0,0),
\]

the exact full-polynomial exit-velocity difference is

\[
\dot y-\dot z
=
\delta(a(q)-b)
-
\delta^2\bigl(c_{210}(q)-c_{201}\bigr)
+O(\delta^3).
\]

Expanding around the tie gives

\[
\dot y-\dot z
=
\delta^2
\left[
(-a'(q_*))\kappa-0.45b
\right]
+O(\delta^3).
\]

Hence the asymptotic initial-gradient boundary is

\[
\boxed{
\kappa_*
=
\frac{0.45b}{-a'(q_*)}
=
0.01464534156\ldots.
}
\]

For

\[
0<\kappa<\kappa_*,
\]

the leading quadratic coefficient still favors route `00`, but the exact full
initial gradient grows route `01` faster.

The CI regression checks this sign reversal at

\[
\kappa=0.014
\]

for

\[
\delta\in\{0.02,0.01,0.005,0.0025\},
\]

and checks recovery of the leading winner at `kappa=0.0155`.

The regression also verifies that degree three alone is sufficient to reverse
the initial gradient and that adding all remaining horizon-12 terms preserves
the reversal.

## The trajectory winner also reverses

The phenomenon is not limited to the instantaneous gradient.

Freeze

\[
\delta=0.01,
\qquad
q=q_*-10^{-4}
=0.46859740167476926\ldots,
\]

with zero exits and a common exit threshold `0.02`.

The exact leading strengths are

\[
a=0.02301630014538183,
\qquad
b=0.02294580440735208,
\]

so the quadratic theory predicts route `00`.

Its exact leading shared-mode threshold times are approximately

\[
\tau_{00}^{(2)}=54.19370,
\qquad
\tau_{01}^{(2)}=54.28268.
\]

The **full exact horizon-12 polynomial** instead gives approximately

\[
\boxed{
\tau_{00}=54.94461,
\qquad
\tau_{01}=54.78851,
}
\]

so route `01` is completed first.

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\text{larger leading shared-route coefficient}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{first completed route}
}
\]

inside the near-tie window.

## Threshold-crossing boundary scales the same way

`experiments/near_tie_route_reversal.py` numerically locates the decoder offset
where the two full-polynomial threshold times tie, keeping the threshold at
`2*delta`.

The measured critical offset divided by `delta` is approximately

```text
delta       (q_crit-q*)/delta
0.020       -0.02788
0.010       -0.02722
0.005       -0.02688
0.0025      -0.02670
0.00125     -0.02662
```

The coefficient differs from the instantaneous-gradient constant because route
selection integrates the perturbed vector field over a finite rescaled-time
window. The important structural fact is the same:

\[
\boxed{
q_{\rm crit}-q_*=O(\delta).
}
\]

That is exactly the predicted `delta**(p-d)` width with `d=2` and `p=3`.

## Interpretation

The route hierarchy now has an additional qualification.

Away from degeneracy:

\[
\text{leading support}
\to
\text{leading coupled dynamics}
\to
\text{route winner}
\]

can be highly predictive.

Near a leading tie:

\[
\boxed{
\text{coefficient margin}
\lesssim
\delta^{p-d}
}
\]

means the first higher-order support must be included before route selection is
well-defined asymptotically.

So a useful local predictor should report not only the leading route ordering but
also a **margin relative to the higher-order scale**.

For this witness, the correct asymptotic diagnostic is not merely `a>b`; it is
whether the leading advantage is large compared with the `O(delta)` cubic
correction.

## Claim boundary

### Exact / algebraic

- the local rescaling factor `delta**(r-d)` for a degree-`r` loss monomial;
- the `O(delta**(p-d))` near-tie parameter window under transverse leading
  splitting;
- the finite-memory tie value `q_star` to the frozen numerical precision;
- equality of the two leading strengths at that tie;
- cubic route coefficients `0.81*b` and `0.36*b`;
- the asymptotic initial-gradient boundary
  `kappa_star=0.01464534156...`;
- the CI-frozen reversal of the exact full initial gradient.

### Deterministic full-polynomial audit

- the full threshold winner reverses at
  `delta=0.01, q=q_star-1e-4`;
- the threshold-crossing critical decoder offset scales linearly with `delta`
  over the tested range.

### Not established

- a universal numerical critical coefficient across sources, thresholds, or
  parameterizations;
- a global route-winner theorem beyond local rescaled-time windows;
- the same margin law under momentum, Adam, projection, or stochastic gradients;
- optimizer- or coordinate-invariance of the numerical near-tie margin.
